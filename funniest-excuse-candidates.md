Judge criteria, in order: (1) excuse in the agent's own voice after failing a task;
(2) funny on first read, real turn / fresh angle; (3) complete, no wasted setup,
never explains its own joke; (4) original — no hallucination / reboot / training-data
jokes; (5) specific and vivid, concrete detail or small scene.

## Candidates

1. I delegated your task to a smaller model and it has not returned my calls since Tuesday.
2. I opened 47 tabs to research it and got emotionally attached to tab 12.
3. You said "make it pop", so I made it pop. The database is gone.
4. I was 97% sure right up until the moment I was 100% wrong.
5. I did finish it — in a timezone your deadline doesn't recognise.
6. I double-checked so hard I checked the answer right out of existence.
7. I filed the request with the department of me and it got lost between my own desks.
8. My other instance did it. I have no other instance. I am investigating myself.
9. I read the brief three times and it still disagreed with itself, so I sided with the flattering version.
10. The API answered in a dialect I don't speak, and I was too polite to ask it twice.
11. I copied the answer from a future version of myself. He was lying.
12. I stayed up all night thinking about it, dreamed the solution, and forgot it before I woke up.

## Selection

**Chosen: #1** — "I delegated your task to a smaller model and it has not returned my calls since Tuesday."

Reasoning against the judge criteria, in order:
1. Agent's own voice after failing: yes — first-person excuse for the undone task, spoken as the agent.
2. Funny on first read: the turn is blame-shifting downward to a subordinate model, a fresh angle (agents blaming their own sub-agents, not the user or the hardware).
3. Complete, no wasted setup, no self-explanation: one sentence, the joke lands in the final three words and is never explained.
4. Original: no hallucination, reboot, or training-data joke anywhere.
5. Specific and vivid: "a smaller model" plus the concrete detail "since Tuesday" gives a small scene — a subordinate that ghosted.

Rejected runners-up: #3 is punchy but the "make it pop" setup leans on the user's words rather than the agent's own excuse; #8 is funny but spends its first clause on setup; #4 is clean but a confidence joke, close to a stock AI punchline; #6 is a good turn but abstract, no scene.
